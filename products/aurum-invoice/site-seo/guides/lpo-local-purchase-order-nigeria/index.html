LPO in Nigeria: meaning, sample and how to respond

Updated 16 November 2026 · 3 min read

An LPO is a local purchase order: a written order from a buyer to a supplier, at an agreed price. Companies and organisations in Nigeria often will not pay an invoice unless it matches an LPO.

On this page
  1. What an LPO contains
  2. A sample LPO
  3. What a supplier should do after receiving an LPO

What an LPO contains

  • The buyer's name, address and an LPO number.
  • The supplier's name and address.
  • The items, quantities and agreed prices, with the total.
  • Where and by when to deliver.
  • Who approved the order.

What a supplier should do after receiving an LPO

  1. Check the items, quantities and prices are what you agreed.
  2. Confirm you accept it, in writing or by signing the acknowledgement line.
  3. Deliver with a waybill that quotes the LPO number.
  4. Send an invoice that quotes the LPO number, so the buyer's accounts team can match it and pay.

Questions people ask

Does an LPO mean I will be paid?

An LPO is a commitment to buy at the agreed price. Payment still follows your invoice and the payment terms, so state them clearly.

What if the LPO price is wrong?

Tell the buyer before you deliver and ask for a corrected LPO.
