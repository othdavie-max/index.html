What makes a good quotation

  • A valid-until date. Prices change. A quote should not last forever.
  • Itemised prices. Customers trust a breakdown more than one lump sum.
  • What is included, and what is not. Say if delivery, installation or materials are extra.
  • A timeline for when you can deliver.
  • The deposit you need to start, so there is no awkward conversation later.
  • A clear way to accept. A signature or a written "yes".

Quotation, estimate or proforma?

These are close cousins. A quotation is a firm price offer. An estimate is a best guess that may change. A proforma invoice is a preliminary invoice sent before the sale is complete, often for the customer to arrange payment. See our guide on proforma invoices.

From quote to invoice

Once the customer accepts, copy the same line items into an invoice with a new invoice number, and reference the quote number. This keeps the price exactly as agreed. In Aurum, an accepted quotation becomes an invoice in one tap, with nothing retyped.

Sending it so it gets accepted

Reply fast, send it as a clean PDF or a private link, and follow up politely after a day or two if you hear nothing. A quote that looks premium is also easier to say yes to, because it signals you are serious.

Questions people ask

How long should a quotation be valid?

Seven to thirty days is common, depending on how quickly your costs change. State the date clearly on the quote.

Can a customer hold me to my quotation?

A clear, accepted quotation is a record of what you both agreed. Keep a copy and put key terms in writing. Ask a lawyer if you need legal advice on a specific contract.

Should I ask for a deposit on a quotation?

For custom work or large orders, yes. It shows the customer is serious and covers your first costs.
