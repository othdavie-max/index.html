VAT on invoices in Nigeria: how to show it

Updated 12 November 2026 · 2 min read

Value Added Tax (VAT) is a tax added to the price of many goods and services. Whether you must charge it depends on your business. If you do, it should appear clearly on the invoice. Here are the basics.

The key point first

About tax. Tax rules for small businesses in Nigeria changed with the 2025 tax reforms, and they depend on your business type and turnover. This guide is general information, not tax advice. Confirm your own situation with a registered accountant or the tax authority (FIRS, now moving to the Nigeria Revenue Service).

What VAT is

VAT is a consumption tax. A VAT-registered business adds it to what it sells, collects it from the customer, and pays it over to the tax authority. The standard rate in Nigeria has been 7.5%. Check the current rate and exemptions for your goods or services before you rely on this.

How to show VAT on an invoice

  • List your items and the subtotal before VAT.
  • Add a separate line: VAT (7.5%) with the amount.
  • Show the total including VAT as the amount due.
  • If you are VAT-registered, put your TIN or VAT number on the invoice.

A worked example

DescriptionAmount
Brand design service₦200,000.00
Subtotal₦200,000.00
VAT (7.5%)₦15,000.00
Total due₦215,000.00

To get VAT: multiply the subtotal by 0.075. Here, 200,000 × 0.075 = 15,000. This is a sample calculation only, not tax advice.

Prices with VAT already included

If your price already includes VAT, say so ("VAT inclusive") so the customer does not think it will be added later. To find the VAT inside a total, divide the total by 1.075 to get the price before VAT, then subtract.

Questions people ask

What is the VAT rate in Nigeria?

It has been 7.5%. Check the current rate and any exemptions with the tax authority or an accountant, as rules can change.

Do small businesses have to charge VAT?

It depends on your business and turnover, and the rules changed with the 2025 tax reforms. Ask an accountant.

Should VAT appear as its own line?

Yes, if you charge it. Showing it separately makes the invoice clear and easier to account for.
