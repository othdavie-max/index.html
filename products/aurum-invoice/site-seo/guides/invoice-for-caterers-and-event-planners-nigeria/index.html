How caterers and event planners in Nigeria should invoice

Updated 12 November 2026 · 2 min read

Food and event jobs cost real money before the day arrives: ingredients, hiring, transport. Your invoice should show what you are providing, how many people it is for, and when you must be paid.

What to put on the invoice

  • The event: name, date, time and venue.
  • Headcount: the number of guests the price is based on, and the cut-off date for changing it.
  • Menu or package: per-head price, with the menu items summarised or attached.
  • Extras as separate lines: chairs and tables, decoration, cake, service staff, drinks, rentals, transport.
  • Delivery and setup time and who is responsible for the venue.

Deposit and payment terms

Take a deposit of 50% when the booking is confirmed, since you buy food and book staff early. A common arrangement is the balance 3 to 7 days before the event. State the final headcount date: after it, guest numbers can go up but not down. Put your cancellation rule on the invoice, for example what happens to the deposit if the event is cancelled within a week. Our guide on getting paid on time applies strongly to events, where late payment is common.

Problems a clear invoice prevents

  • Guest numbers creeping up. The invoice states the headcount the price is for and the deadline to change it.
  • "I thought chairs were included." Every extra is its own line.
  • Being paid after the event. The balance is due beforehand, in writing.
  • Disputes about the menu. The invoice lists what was agreed.

Questions people ask

How much deposit should a caterer ask for?

Fifty percent is common, so your ingredient and staff costs are covered before the event.

Should the balance be paid before the event?

Yes, ideally a few days before. It removes the risk of chasing a happy but busy host afterwards.

How do I price per head on an invoice?

Show the price per person, the number of guests and the total, so changes in numbers are easy to adjust.
