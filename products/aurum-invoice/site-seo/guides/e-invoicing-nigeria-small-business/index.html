E-invoicing in Nigeria: what small businesses should know

Updated 16 November 2026 · 4 min read

Nigeria is bringing in a government e-invoicing system for businesses, starting with the largest. Here is what it is, who it applies to now, and what a small business should do today.

On this page
  1. What it is
  2. Who it applies to, and when
  3. What a small business should do now
  4. Where Aurum fits
  5. Sources

What it is

The Nigeria Revenue Service (NRS) runs a national e-invoicing system, also called the Electronic Fiscal System, delivered through a Merchant Buyer Solution. Qualifying businesses must send their invoice details to the NRS system in a set format, and the invoice receives an official confirmation.

Who it applies to, and when

  • Large taxpayers (reported as ₦5 billion turnover and above): the NRS set 31 July 2026 as the deadline to adopt the system, with enforcement afterwards.
  • Medium taxpayers (reported as ₦1 billion to ₦5 billion): reported go-live from 1 July 2026, with enforcement reported for early 2027.
  • Smaller businesses: reported to follow later, with 1 July 2027 given for the next group.

These dates come from news reports and tax-firm summaries, and they can change. Check the NRS website for the official notice.

What a small business should do now

  • Find out from an accountant or the NRS whether, and from when, the system applies to your turnover.
  • Keep clean invoices with the customer's full details and your tax identification number.
  • If you supply a large company, expect them to ask for invoices that meet the system's requirements.
