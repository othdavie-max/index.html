An LPO and purchase order maker for small businesses

Updated 16 November 2026 · 3 min read

A purchase order, often called an LPO in Nigeria, is your written order to a supplier at an agreed price. With Aurum you make one on your phone with your logo, the deliver-by date and your delivery address, and the supplier acknowledges it.

On this page
  1. What goes on an LPO
  2. Why order in writing
  3. From LPO to invoice to receipt

What goes on an LPO

  • Your business: name, address and contact, so the supplier knows who is ordering.
  • The supplier: name and address.
  • The items: description, quantity and the agreed unit price, with the total.
  • Delivery: where to deliver and the deliver-by date.
  • Acknowledgement: a line for the supplier to confirm they accept the order.

Why order in writing

A written order protects both sides. You have the price you agreed. The supplier has proof of what you asked for. When the invoice arrives you can check it against the LPO line by line, and when a delivery is short you know exactly what was promised.

From LPO to invoice to receipt

The usual order is: you send an LPO, the supplier delivers with a waybill, then sends an invoice that quotes the LPO number, and you pay and receive a receipt. If you also sell, your own customers' LPOs come to you the same way. Put their LPO number on your invoice so their accounts team can match it.

Questions people ask

What does LPO stand for?

LPO stands for local purchase order. It is the same idea as a purchase order, and in Nigeria the short name is more common.

Do I need an LPO for a small purchase?

No rule says you must, but writing down what you ordered and the agreed price costs a minute and avoids arguments.

Can I make an LPO from my phone?
